Live agent mode is a continuous, low-latency audio and video session with Bower. Unlike chat, where you type a question and wait for the answer, live mode is conversational. You can talk through an experiment, describe what's on an instrument display, or ask follow-up questions without stopping to type.

Live mode runs on your phone and on [Bower Glasses](/bower-glasses/overview), not on a computer. With video enabled, Bower can see what your camera sees and respond to visual context: "What's the reading on that display?" or "Does this sample look contaminated to you?" are reasonable things to ask.

## When to use it

- You're mid-experiment and want to talk through your observations
- You need to describe something visual that would be difficult to type
- You want Bower to track key observations while you work
- You want a real-time second opinion before moving to the next step
- You want hands-free audio + video clips of a procedure as it happens

## How to start a live session

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Bower app">
    Tap **+** in the tab bar and turn the dial to **Live**. To run a protocol, open it and tap **Execute Protocol** instead.

    Allow microphone and camera access when asked, then start talking.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Bower Glasses">
    Press and hold the glasses button, or say *"Go live"*. Bower sees through the glasses camera and answers through the speakers. See [Live and protocols with Bower Glasses](/bower-glasses/live-and-protocols).
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Phone browser">
    If you use Bower in your phone's browser instead of the app, open Bower chat and tap the waveform button beside the send (arrow) button. Its tooltip reads **Live conversation**.

    You can also tap **Go live with Bower** on the **Capture** tab in voice or video mode.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Note>
Live doesn't run on a computer. Get the [Bower app](/getting-started/mobile) on your phone, then start Live from its capture menu.
</Note>

## Video awareness

With video enabled, Bower processes your camera feed in real time. It can:

- Read values from instrument displays you point the camera at
- Describe what it sees when asked
- Flag visual anomalies if you ask it to watch for them

Bower doesn't act on what it sees unprompted. It responds when you ask.

## Capturing notes during a session

You can ask Bower to capture what it sees or hears at any point during a live session:

- **"Take a snapshot"**: Bower captures the current camera frame and saves it as an attachment in your workspace.
- **"Create a note from what we just discussed"**: Bower creates a note with the key points from your conversation.
- You can also take a photo yourself with the shutter button (below).

### Taking a photo yourself

In the Bower app, tap **Take a photo** on the live screen to save a photo yourself.

In a live session in your phone's browser, a round **Take photo** button appears just above the controls whenever your camera is on. It's hidden while Bower is in standby.

1. Point the camera at what you want to keep.
2. Tap **Take photo**. The button shows a spinner while the photo saves, then a check mark.

The photo is saved as an attachment in your workspace. It's a full frame from your camera feed, so it's sharper than the smaller frames Bower sees during the conversation. If you're running a protocol, the photo is linked to that run.

Bower is told about the photo without being asked to reply, so it doesn't stop to comment. It knows you took one and, during a protocol, which step you were on, so later in the session you can refer to it, for example *"which step was that last photo for?"*

If the photo can't be saved, you'll see "Photo didn't save. Try again." and nothing is added to your workspace.

## Recording video clips

You can ask Bower to record short audio + video clips of your bench work hands-free. While you're in a live session, just say what you want and Bower records it.

**To start recording**, say something like:

- *"Record this"*
- *"Start recording"*
- *"Capture a clip of this"*
- *"Film this for me"*

Bower confirms ("Recording now.") and a red **REC** indicator appears on screen for as long as the recording is active.

**To stop**, say:

- *"Stop recording"*
- *"Stop the recording"*
- *"End the recording"*

The word "recording" is required. A bare "stop", "cut" or "that's enough" doesn't end the clip, because people say those while filming.

Bower confirms and the clip is saved to your workspace's attachments as MP4, where it plays back inline.

| Setting               | Value                                                              |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Maximum length        | **2 minutes**; Bower stops automatically if you don't               |
| Concurrent recordings | One per session                                                    |
| If your network drops | Saved up to the drop, on a best-effort basis (see below)            |
| Where it's saved      | Workspace attachments, MIME type `video/mp4`                       |
| Storage cost          | Counts against your standard workspace storage quota               |

If the connection drops while a recording is open, Bower saves what it received up to that point to your attachments when the session ends. Bower can't confirm this out loud, because the session is already gone. The saved clip has a 60-second limit to finish processing; if it takes longer, that clip isn't saved. Check your attachments after reconnecting.

<Note>
**Recordings are intentionally slideshow-quality** (1–2 fps for the video track, full-quality audio). This keeps file sizes small for everyday lab observation. It's not for high-motion footage.
</Note>

<Info>
**Privacy:** on your phone, the **REC indicator is visible on the live screen** for as long as a recording is active, both for your awareness and so colleagues nearby know they may be on camera. Bower Glasses have no display, so the indicator shows on the live screen in the Bower app when it's open. In a background *"Hey Bower"* session there's no on-screen indicator, so tell people nearby before you record. Clips are only visible to people who can already see your other workspace attachments.
</Info>

Bower is tuned to be conservative and only start recording when you explicitly ask. Phrases that mention "record" but mean something else, such as *"recall the protocol"*, *"record my thoughts in my notebook"* or *"what's the record temperature"*, won't trigger a recording.

## Standby mode

Standby is available in live sessions in your phone's browser.

When you need to pause, to have a private conversation, take a call or step away, put Bower in standby.

**In standby, Bower's servers receive no audio or video from your device.** The live stream to Bower is fully paused.

- **To enter standby:** Tap the standby button, or say **"Go to standby"** or **"Goodbye Bower"**
- **To wake Bower:** Say **"Hello Bower"**, or tap the standby button again to resume manually

The wake word **"Hello Bower"** is detected by your browser's built-in speech recognition, so your microphone stays on in standby. In Chrome and Edge, the browser sends that audio to its own speech service (Google or Microsoft) to recognize the phrase. Bower's servers don't receive it. Only once the wake word is recognized does the live stream to Bower resume.

That means you can leave Bower active without streaming to Bower the whole time: keep your phone in your pocket with headphones, or leave it on the lab bench while you take a call. Standby is useful for long lab sessions where you want Bower available but not always active.

## Execution mode (running a protocol)

When you start a live session and ask Bower to run one of your saved protocols, the live agent enters **execution mode**. Bower walks you through the protocol step by step and tracks your progress, so you can keep both hands on the bench.

### Starting a protocol

- *"Run the **&lt;name&gt;** protocol"*
- *"Start the **&lt;name&gt;** protocol"*
- *"Let's do the **&lt;name&gt;** protocol"*

If more than one protocol matches what Bower heard, it lists them and asks which one you meant.

### Step cards

Each step appears as a card at the bottom of the live screen. Swipe between cards to look ahead or back; the current step is centered as you advance. Tap **Mark done** to finish a step without speaking, or **Watch** to play that step in the protocol's reference video, when it has one.

Tap **Details** to open the full step: its formatted instructions (lists, tables, formulas), its materials, and any pictures, videos or files attached to it. Tap a picture or video to view it full screen. Files open in a new tab, so your run keeps going.

### Advancing through steps

Bower marks the current step done when you tell it you're finished. Natural phrasings all work:

- *"Done"* / *"That's done"* / *"Next"*
- *"Move on"* / *"Finished that"*

Bower takes your word for it: it won't ask you to confirm or wait to check on camera.

You can also skip a step:

- *"Skip this one"* / *"We don't need this step"*

### Jumping to a specific step

To go to a particular step, by number or by what it's about, say so:

- *"Jump to step 5"* / *"Go back to the staining step"* / *"Let's do the gas setup now"*

A clear step number, such as *"Jump to step 5"*, moves the run straight there.

Because landing on the wrong step mid-procedure is disruptive, **a jump to a step you name by what it's about is confirmed first**. Bower works out which step you meant and reads it back as a quick check, for example *"Go to step 4, Set up gas lines?"*, and waits. Say *"yes"*, *"do it"*, or *"go ahead"* and Bower makes the jump. Say no, or name a different step, and nothing jumps until you confirm.

Marking a step done, skipping, and going back all stay instant. If Bower isn't sure which step you meant, it asks you to say which one rather than guessing.

### Pausing and resuming

- *"Pause for a sec"* / *"Hold on"*: Bower stops the running clock and stays quiet until you resume
- *"Resume"* / *"Where were we?"*: Bower announces the current step again and continues
- *"That's done"* / *"We're finished"* (after the last step): Bower ends the run and returns to general live mode

### Undoing the last step

When you mark the last step done, Bower finishes the protocol. If that *"done"* was misheard, you have **10 minutes**, in the same live session, to take it back:

1. Say *"Go back"* or *"Undo"*, or name a step, such as *"Go back to step 6"*.
2. Bower always asks first, for example *"Reopen the finished protocol at step 6, Image the gel?"*
3. Say *"yes"* and the run reopens at that step. Say no and it stays finished.

Only your voice can reopen a run, not a tap. A reopened run you leave unfinished is recorded as **Ended early**. After 10 minutes, or for a run that ended early, start a new run.

### Timers

Inside a protocol you can set a wall-clock timer for the current step:

- *"Remind me in 30 minutes to take the gel out"*
- *"Set a 5-minute timer"*
- *"Buzz me when the incubation is done in 10 minutes"*

Bower reads the duration back so you can catch a misheard time, then quietly waits. When the timer fires, Bower speaks the reminder.

Timers belong to the **current step**, and moving on clears them. If you want a multi-step reminder, set it on the step it should fire during.

### Reporting deviations

If something goes wrong or you do something differently than the protocol says, tell Bower. Examples:

- *"I skipped the centrifuge step"*
- *"I used 50 microliters, not 100"*
- *"I waited 10 minutes instead of 30"*
- *"That tube touched the bench"*

Bower records the deviation against the current step, with its kind (such as timing, a parameter or a sterile breach), so you have a record of what actually happened when you troubleshoot a failed run.

## Remembering something for next time

When you tell Bower something worth keeping during a protocol run, such as *"remember that we use 50 µL here"* or *"make a note that the buffer was cold"*, Bower writes it into a **note** anchored to that run and tells you so: *"Got it, putting that in a note for this run."* The note appears on that run's record straight away and is searchable like any other note. See [What is on a run record?](/organisation/protocols#what-is-on-a-run-record)

Bower does not carry these notes into future runs. It won't read a tip back to you when you reach the same step next time, so if a change should apply to every run, edit the protocol itself.

If you ask for a note without saying what it should contain (*"take a note"*), Bower starts a voice note instead and records what you dictate next.

<Note>
Bower AI annotations have been retired. Bower used to capture per-step tips while you ran a protocol and bring them up on later runs of the same protocol. It no longer captures or uses them, and the **Bower AI annotations** entry is gone from the Protocol detail menu.
</Note>

## Visual watchers

You can ask Bower to react to a visual cue during any live session, not only while running a protocol: "every time you see X, say Y". Tell Bower what to watch for and what to say, and it checks the camera in the background.

### How to set one up

Just say it in plain words:

- *"Every time you see a thumbs up, say 'cool, man.'"*
- *"Let me know when the gel turns pink."*
- *"Watch for the color change and tell me."*
- *"Alert me if my hand touches the bench."*

Bower confirms it's watching and starts checking camera frames every few seconds. When it spots a high-confidence match, Bower speaks the response you chose.

### Limits

- **Up to 3 watchers per session.** Past the limit, Bower tells you and asks you to clear one first. Voice-clear with *"forget about the thumbs up"* or *"stop watching for the gel"*.
- **About a 15-second cooldown per watcher.** If the cue stays on camera, Bower doesn't repeat itself until the cooldown has passed.
- **Watchers reset on disconnect.** End the live session and the watchers go with it. Set them up again in the next session if you want them back.
- **Each watcher costs a small amount.** Bower uses a separate AI model to check frames, so active watchers add a per-session cost. Clear them when you're done.

### What works well

Concrete, visually unambiguous cues, such as *"a researcher's thumbs-up gesture"*, *"the centrifuge display reaching 10000 rpm"* or *"the gel turning pink"*.

Vague targets and things the camera can't see work less well:

- *"good things"* is too abstract to recognize.
- *"when I'm done"* depends on your intent, not on anything visible.
- *"the temperature reaching 37 °C"* works only if the readout is on camera.

### Asking Bower what it's watching for

Ask *"what are you watching for?"* or *"what cues did I set up?"*. Bower lists the active watchers and how many times each has fired.

## What's saved

During a live session, notes, snapshots, and video clips are only saved when you explicitly ask Bower to capture them, or when you take a photo yourself with the shutter button.

A watcher firing saves nothing to your workspace. Bower speaks the response and carries on.

All captured notes, snapshots, and clips are searchable immediately and play back inline in the attachment viewer.
