---
title: "Protocols: structured, versioned lab procedures"
description: "Build lab protocols in Bower as sections of numbered steps with typed values, a customizable sidebar, capture steps and versions that lock once approved, then see every run with its step times and captures."
---

A protocol in Bower is your lab's own step-by-step procedure for a method: "how we run PCR in this lab", not the published method. Protocols live in their own library, and you can tag, link and search them like anything else in Bower.

A protocol is made of:

- **Steps grouped in sections**, lettered and numbered so everyone refers to the same step.
- **Typed values** in each step, such as temperatures, volumes and durations, that Bower can check.
- **Capture steps** that say what to record at that point: a photo, a video or a voice note.
- **A sidebar** for parameters, materials, recipes, safety notes and other reference material.
- **Versions** that lock once approved.
- **Runs**, a record of each time someone worked through the protocol.

To change a protocol, edit its steps in a draft version. Bower writes the protocol's plain text from the steps, for search and for AI apps, so you never edit that text directly.

## How do I create a protocol?

1. Click **Protocols** in the left rail.
2. Click **New protocol**. The **New protocol** window asks **How would you like to start?**
3. Choose one:
   - **Attach a file.** Pick a PDF or Word (.docx) document. Bower reads the steps out of it, and you correct anything it gets wrong.
   - **Start from scratch.** You get a draft with one section, **Procedure**, ready for its first step, and empty **Acceptance criteria**, **Materials & equipment**, **Safety** and **Troubleshooting** sections in the sidebar. Rename or remove any of them.

Create and edit protocols on the web. The Bower app on your phone is for opening and running them.

### Can I turn a note into a protocol?

Yes. Open the note, click **⋮**, then **Convert to Protocol**. Bower opens the protocol page, lays your text out as steps, and keeps the text as the protocol's source file, `Original note.md`.

### What happens while Bower reads my document?

The page shows **Reading your document**, or **Reading your protocol** for a converted note, and the library shows **Importing**. A long protocol takes a few minutes; you can leave the page while it works.

If Bower can't find enough procedural detail, or reading stops partway, the page says why. What you can do next depends on where the protocol came from:

| Made from | What you can do |
| --- | --- |
| A file | If Bower found too little detail, ask in the chat to try again and extract whatever it can. If the import stopped, delete the protocol and import the file again. |
| Your own text, such as a converted note | Nothing is lost: your text is kept. Click **Try again**, or **Keep it as a note** to turn it back into a note with your text. Only people who can edit the protocol see these buttons. |

## What's on a protocol page?

At the top are the title and the **version bar**, which shows the version's state, such as **draft** or **approved**, and its name. Click the name to rename the version, for example "Rev C".

Once a protocol has more than one version, the header shows the number and state together, such as **● v2 approved**. The dot is green for the version runs use, blue for a draft and gray for an earlier version. To open another version, click it in **Lineage** in the sidebar. While you're on the version runs use and a draft exists, a line under the header says so, with a link to open the draft.

A protocol opens on its newest version, which may be a draft. **Execute Protocol** appears only on the version runs use, so on a draft, open that version from **Lineage** first.

Below the version bar:

- **Method** holds the sections (**A**, **B**, **C**) and their steps (**A.1**, **A.2**, **B.1**). Steps outside a section are under **Not in a section**. **Expand all** and **Collapse all** open or close every section.
- **The sidebar** sits beside the method. See [What goes in the protocol sidebar?](#what-goes-in-the-protocol-sidebar)

Each step has a type icon (**Do**, **Wait**, **Hold**, **Check** or **Note**), a title, its text, and sometimes a note on why it matters. Attached pictures, videos and files appear below the step.

## How do I edit steps and sections?

In a draft:

- Click **Add step** or **Add section**.
- Drag a step by its handle to reorder it or move it to another section, or use **Move up**, **Move down** and **Move into**.
- Click a step's icon to change its type.
- Hover over a step and click **Remove step** to delete it.
- Use a section's actions to merge it into the one above, or remove it.
- Click **Add media** to attach a picture, video or file to a step.

Changes save automatically. **Undo** and **Redo** are at the top of the page, or press Cmd+Z and Shift+Cmd+Z.

## What are typed values?

A typed value is an amount with a unit, such as *37 °C*, *5 mL* or *10 min*, and how precise it is. Typed values appear as highlighted chips in the step.

When Bower imports a protocol, it marks the values it finds. A value it isn't sure about has a warning color and a dashed underline until someone checks it. Check it against your source before you rely on it.

Click a value to see the wording it came from (**The document said …**) and correct its amount, unit and precision: **Exact**, **About**, **Range** or **Until**. If no precision is selected, the document didn't state one. Click **Remove: not a value** if it isn't a value.

To add a value, click **Insert a typed value** in the step's toolbar, or type `/` and choose **Value**.

Reagents and equipment Bower recognizes are underlined. Hover over one to see what Bower took it to be.

## What are capture steps?

A capture step asks the person running the protocol to record a **photo**, **video** or **voice note** at that point, such as a photo of a gel.

In a draft, hover over a step, click **Add capture**, and choose the type. A step asks for one capture at most; choose **Nothing** to clear it. Bower also marks capture steps when your imported document asks for a photo, video or voice note.

## What goes in the protocol sidebar?

The sidebar holds reference material for the steps. Below **Lineage** and **Runs**, it starts with **Parameters**, the values your lab chooses for each run:

- **Options**: a list to choose from. Press an option to make it the default.
- **Range**: a number with a minimum, maximum, unit and default.
- **Text**: free text, with an optional default.

Parameters stay at the top of that group and can't be moved or removed as a section. The built-in sections below them are:

- **Acceptance criteria**: what a successful run looks like.
- **Materials & equipment**: what you need, with vendor and catalog number.
- **Recipes**: buffers and mixes the steps refer to.
- **Safety**: hazards and precautions.
- **Troubleshooting**: problems and their fixes.
- **Review notes**: things in an imported document Bower thinks are worth checking.
- **Other details**: anything else, as a label and a value.

You can add your own sections too, such as "Storage" or "Waste disposal". Empty sections are hidden except in a draft.

### How do I customize the sidebar?

In a draft:

- **Add a section.** Click **Add section** at the foot of the sidebar, then pick a built-in section or **Custom section…**.
- **Rename a custom section.** Click its name.
- **Reorder sections.** Drag a section by the handle on its header. With a keyboard, focus the handle, press Space, use the arrow keys, and press Space again.
- **Remove a section.** Hover over its header and click **✕**. Bower asks first if it has entries. **Materials & equipment** and **Recipes** can be removed only when empty.

## How do versions and approval work?

When a version is ready for the bench, click **Approve this version**. You can give the approved state your lab's own name.

<Warning>
Approval can't be undone. The version's values, steps and sidebar are fixed, so every run recorded against it matches what the page said at the time.
</Warning>

To change an approved protocol, click **New version**. Bower copies it into a draft, and runs keep using the approved version until you approve the new one.

**Lineage**, at the top of the sidebar, lists every version, newest first, with where each came from (**From v2**, or **Imported**), who created it and when. It ends with the file the protocol was imported from. Click a version's row to open it. A long history shows the newest three versions, the version runs use and the one you're viewing, with a **Show 5 earlier versions** row that opens the rest in place.

To delete a version, open it and click **⋮**, then **Delete version**. You can delete a draft, or an earlier approved version nobody ran. You can't delete a version that has runs, the version runs use, or a protocol's only version. Version numbers aren't reused.

## Protocol runs

A run is one time someone worked through a protocol, usually hands-free with [Live](/ai-and-search/live-agent-mode#execution-mode-running-a-protocol). Bower records which version was run, when each step was marked done or skipped, and what was captured. Anyone who can read a protocol can see its runs. Only the person who started a run can continue it.

### What do the run labels mean?

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Open · step 3 of 6 · last activity 14:02** | In progress at step 3. 14:02 is the last recorded activity. |
| **Paused · step 3 of 6 · last activity 14:02** | Paused at step 3. |
| **Completed · 6 of 6 (1 skipped)** | Every step was done or skipped. |
| **Ended early · 2 of 6** | Closed before the last step: ended, replaced by a new run, or closed after Live lost the connection. |

Each run also shows its version, such as **v2**, or **Earlier** for runs from before the protocol had versions.

### Can a finished run change?

No. A **Completed** or **Ended early** run is a fixed record; start a new run to do the protocol again. The one exception: in Live, you can [undo a misheard last step within 10 minutes](/ai-and-search/live-agent-mode#undoing-the-last-step).

### How do I see the runs of a protocol?

In the protocol library, click a protocol's run count, such as **3 runs · 1 open**, to show its runs underneath, newest first. Protocols with an open run show them already.

- **Active runs** in the toolbar lists only protocols with an open run, and under each one shows only its open runs (**Open** or **Paused**). The protocol's run count still counts every run.
- The **Protocol** filter shows one protocol's runs. **View all in Protocols** on a protocol's page does the same.

### How do I see when a protocol was last run?

The library's **Last run** column shows the most recent run in your local time, such as **Today, 09:12**. For an open run, it's the last activity. Click the column header to sort by it.

### What is on a run record?

Click a run to open its record. The header shows who ran it, when, which version, how many captures and how long it took.

- **Steps** lists the steps as they were run, under the protocol page's own headings (**Section A**, **Section B**) and labels (**A.1**, **A.2**, **B.1**). Each shows its captures (click one to open it) and when it was marked, such as **Done · 10:06:41** or **Skipped · 10:07:05**.
  - If Live asked for a photo or video on a step and it hadn't reached Bower when the session ended, the step shows **Photo not received** or **Video not received**. The step's status doesn't change, and the line goes away if the capture arrives later.
  - A finished step shows how long it took, such as **took 3 min 05 s**. Once the version has at least three other completed runs, it adds the usual time, such as **usually 45 s**, in amber when the step took at least twice as long and a minute or more over.
  - The step's amounts, such as **5 mL** or **37 °C**, show as small chips, leaving out any the step title already says. After six, the rest are counted, such as **+2 more**.
  - **Photo not received** or **Video not received** means Bower asked for a photo or clip on that step during a Live session, and it had not reached Bower when the session ended. If it arrives later, the line goes away.
- **Run analysis**, at the foot of the record, holds the run's notes and captures that belong to no step, in the order they were taken. **View analysis** in the **Summary** jumps there.
  - A note reads in place: who wrote it, when, **Edited** and the time if it changed later, then its text, with **Show more** on a long one. Images show as a count, such as **2 images**; open the note to see them. Edits made elsewhere show here straight away.
  - **Add a note** adds your own note to the run as a whole, never to the step the run is on, in the same editor and formatting as any note. Click **Add note** to save it as an ordinary note linked to the run, with its own [version history](/capture/version-history). An image or a table alone is enough; a note with no words is titled **Note**. A note added by someone other than the person who ran it shows who added it. Anyone who can edit in the workspace can add a note; guests can only read them.
  - **Dictate** records you; Bower transcribes it and adds the text when you stop.
  - Add images with the toolbar, the slash menu, paste or drag. They upload into the note when you click **Add note**. If one fails, Bower tells you and saves the note without it, so open the note to add it again. Diagrams can be added once the note is saved.
- **Steps done** in the **Summary** counts the steps marked, such as **2 of 3 (1 skipped)**.
- **Transcript** in the **Summary** opens the run's [Live run transcript](/ai-and-search/live-run-transcripts), or reads **Not saved** when there isn't one.
- **Timeline** lists the start, each capture, each step done, and the end, in order.

Times are in your device's time zone; the **Summary** names it. A notice tells you if the protocol has changed since the run.

### Runs and versions on the protocol page

At the top of a protocol's sidebar, **Runs** lists up to five runs of the version you're viewing; turn on **All versions** for the rest. In **Lineage**, a version with runs ends its second line with the count, such as **37 runs**; click it to show that version's runs here without leaving the version you're on.

## Can Bower answer questions about a protocol?

Yes. Ask Bower in the chat, for example "What are the safety notes for the PCR setup protocol?". It reads the steps, capture steps and every sidebar section. See [Bower AI assistant](/ai-and-search/bower-ai-assistant).

## What happens when two people edit the same step?

Bower keeps both changes. A banner appears on the step: click **Review** to compare them, then **Keep mine** or **Use theirs**.

## What if an import misses something?

If an import misses something, such as a table or the materials list, a notice at the top of the page lists what's missing and where in the source, when Bower knows. Check those parts against your original document.

## What happened to protocols written as a document?

Bower used to offer protocols written as a single note. Each one has been converted to steps, with your original text kept as `Original note.md`. Open it from the bottom of the protocol's **Lineage** to check the steps against what you wrote.

## The protocol library

The library lists every protocol in your workspace and works like the workspace's [List view](/organisation/organising-workspace#views). Each row shows the title, collection, versions, [runs](#how-do-i-see-the-runs-of-a-protocol), [last run](#how-do-i-see-when-a-protocol-was-last-run), creator and last modified date. Tags and created date are in the column picker.

Search, sort, and filter by collection, tag, creator or protocol. Select several protocols to act on them together.

### Finding your way back

On a computer, a protocol page shows a trail such as **Protocols / PCR setup**, with its collection beside it. The back arrow returns you to the page you came from: the list with your search, filters and sort as you left them, or a run, note or other page you opened the protocol from.

## Managing and exporting a protocol

A protocol's **⋮** menu holds access, version history, moving, deleting, and export to PDF or Word. See [Protocol menu and export](/organisation/protocol-menu-and-export).

## Linking protocols to notes

To record which protocol a set of observations followed, [link](/organisation/linked-entities) the protocol to those notes, samples or collections.

A note or file captured during a run already shows where it came from, such as **Run 3 · PCR setup**; click it to open that run. Captures from before runs were recorded show **Captured during · PCR setup** and open the protocol.
