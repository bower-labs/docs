---
title: "Protocols: structured, versioned lab procedures"
description: "Build lab protocols in Bower as sections of numbered steps with typed values, a customizable sidebar, capture steps and versions that lock once approved, then see every run with its step times and captures."
---

A protocol in Bower is your lab's own step-by-step procedure for a method. Think "how we run PCR in this lab", not the published method. Protocols live in their own library, separate from your notes, and can be tagged, linked and searched like anything else in Bower.

A Bower protocol is built from parts, not written as one long document:

- **Steps grouped in sections**, lettered and numbered so everyone refers to the same step.
- **Typed values** in each step, such as temperatures, volumes and durations, that Bower can check.
- **Versions** that lock once approved, so a record of a run always matches what the protocol said at the time.
- **A sidebar** beside the steps for materials, recipes, safety notes and anything else your lab wants to keep with the method.
- **Capture steps** that say what the person running the protocol should record: a photo, a video or a voice note.
- **Runs**, a record of each time the protocol was run: which version, when each step was done, and what was captured. See [Protocol runs](#protocol-runs).

## How do I create a protocol?

1. Click **Protocols** in the left rail to open the protocol library.
2. Click **New protocol**, then **Structured protocol**.
3. Choose how to start:
   - **Attach a file.** Pick a PDF or Word (.docx) document. Bower reads the steps out of it and lays them out as a protocol. You can correct anything it gets wrong afterward.
   - **Start from scratch.** You get an empty draft right away. Give it a title and add sections and steps yourself.

### What happens while Bower reads my document?

The protocol page shows **Reading your document** while the import runs. A long protocol takes a few minutes, and you can leave the page while it works. In the library, the protocol shows **Importing** until it's done.

If Bower can't find enough procedural detail to lay out as steps, the page says so and shows its reason. You can ask in the chat to try again and extract whatever it can. If the import fails partway, delete the protocol and import the document again.

## What's on a protocol page?

At the top are the protocol's title and its **version bar**: the state of the version you're viewing (for example **draft** or **approved**) and its name. Click the name to give the version your lab's own name, like "Rev C". A protocol imported from a file links back to it.

Below that:

- **Parameters** are the values your lab chooses each time you run the protocol. Each parameter has a type:
  - **Options**: a list to choose from. Press an option to make it the default.
  - **Range**: a number with a minimum, a maximum, a unit and a default.
  - **Text**: anything you type, with an optional default.
- **Method** holds the sections, lettered **A**, **B**, **C**, and their steps, numbered **A.1**, **A.2**, **B.1**. Steps that don't belong to a section are listed under **Other steps**. Use **Expand all** or **Collapse all** to open or close every section at once.
- **The sidebar** sits beside the method. See [What goes in the protocol sidebar?](#what-goes-in-the-protocol-sidebar)

Each step has its title, its text, and sometimes a short explanation of why it matters. Pictures, videos and files attached to the step appear below it.

## How do I edit steps and sections?

While a version is a draft, you can edit it in place:

- Click **Add step** or **Add section**.
- Drag a step by its handle to reorder it, or to move it into another section. **Move up**, **Move down** and **Move into** do the same without dragging.
- Hover over a step and click **Remove step** to delete it.
- Use a section's actions to merge it into the one above, or to remove it.
- Click **Add media** on a step to attach a picture, video or file. Removing it from the step keeps the file itself.

Changes save automatically. Use **Undo** and **Redo** at the top of the page, or Cmd+Z and Shift+Cmd+Z.

## What are typed values?

A typed value is a quantity Bower understands, not just text: an amount with a unit, such as *37 °C*, *5 mL* or *10 min*, and how precise it is. Typed values appear as highlighted chips in the step.

When Bower imports a protocol, it marks each value it finds and records how confident it is in each one. A value it isn't sure about, that no one has checked yet, is highlighted in a warning color with a dashed underline. Check it against your source before you rely on it.

Click a value to see the wording it came from (**The document said …**) and correct its amount, unit and precision. Precision is one of **Exact**, **About**, **Range** or **Until**. Choose **Range** and a second amount appears after **to**. If no precision is selected, the document didn't state one. Click **Remove: not a value** if Bower marked something that isn't a value.

To add a value by hand, click **Insert a typed value** in the step's toolbar, or type `/` and choose **Value**.

Reagents and equipment Bower recognizes are underlined. Hover over one to see what Bower took it to be.

## What are capture steps?

A capture step tells the person running the protocol to record something at that point: a **photo**, a **video** or a **voice note**. Use one wherever your lab wants evidence of a step, such as a photo of a gel or a video of a technique.

To make a step a capture step in a draft, hover over the step and click **Add capture**, then choose **Photo**, **Video** or **Voice note**. A step asks for one capture at most. Click the capture on the step to change it, or choose **Nothing** to clear it.

When Bower imports a protocol, it marks the steps where your document asks for a photo, a video or a voice note. On an approved version, the capture shows on the step and can't be changed.

## What goes in the protocol sidebar?

The sidebar beside the method holds the reference material that goes with the steps. Bower has these built-in sections, in this default order:

- **Acceptance criteria**: what a successful run looks like.
- **Materials & equipment**: what you need, with vendor and catalog number.
- **Recipes**: buffers and mixes the steps refer to.
- **Safety**: hazards and precautions.
- **Troubleshooting**: what went wrong, and what to do about it.
- **Review notes**: things Bower read in an imported document but thinks are worth checking.
- **Other details**: anything else, as a label and a value.

You can also add sections of your own, such as "Storage" or "Waste disposal".

Each entry in a section has a title, with details below it where the section uses them. A troubleshooting entry, for example, names the problem and then the fix.

When you're reading a protocol, empty sections are hidden. While a version is a draft, every section it has shows, even an empty one, so you can fill it in. To add a section it doesn't have yet, such as **Safety** on a protocol you started from scratch, use **Add section**.

### How do I customize the sidebar?

While a version is a draft, the sidebar is yours to arrange:

- **Add a section.** Click **Add section** at the foot of the sidebar. The menu lists the built-in sections this version doesn't have yet, then **Custom section…**. For a custom section, type its name and press Enter.
- **Rename a custom section.** Click its name and type a new one.
- **Reorder sections.** Drag a section by the handle on its header. With a keyboard, focus the handle, press Space, move it with the arrow keys, and press Space again.
- **Remove a section.** Hover over the section's header and click **✕** (Remove). If the section has entries, Bower asks first and tells you how many entries go with it. An empty section is removed straight away.

**Materials & equipment** and **Recipes** can be removed only when they're empty. Delete their entries first.

Changes to the sidebar apply to the draft you're editing. An approved version keeps the sidebar it was approved with.

## How do versions and approval work?

Every protocol keeps its versions. When a version is ready to use at the bench, click **Approve this version**.

<Warning>
Approving a version can't be undone. Its values, step titles, step order and sidebar are fixed from then on, so a run recorded against it always means what the page said at the time.
</Warning>

When you approve a version, you can name its state if your lab calls it something other than "approved". To change an approved protocol, click **New version**. Bower copies it into a new editable draft, and the approved version stays the one people run until the new one is approved.

Once a protocol has more than one version, a **Versions** section appears in the sidebar, below **Runs**. Its header shows how many versions there are and which one you're looking at, such as **2 · current v1** or **2 · showing v2**. It's folded while you're on the current version and open when you're viewing another one; click the header to open or fold it. Inside, it marks the **current** version and the one you're **showing**; click any version to open it. When a version has been run, its row also shows how many times, such as **· 5 runs**. You can also jump to the list from the protocol's menu with **Version history**.

## Protocol runs

A run is one time someone worked through a protocol, usually hands-free with [Live](/ai-and-search/live-agent-mode#execution-mode-running-a-protocol) on a phone or [Bower Glasses](/bower-glasses/live-and-protocols). Bower keeps a record of every run: which version was run, when each step was marked done, and the photos, videos, voice notes and notes captured along the way.

Anyone who can read a protocol can see its runs. Only the person who ran a run can carry on with it.

### What do the run labels mean?

Each run shows a label that states what Bower recorded, never a guess about whether someone is at the bench right now.

| Label | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Open · step 3 of 6 · last activity 14:02** | The run hasn't finished. Step 3 is the current step, and 14:02 is the last time a step changed or a connected session checked in. |
| **Paused · step 3 of 6 · last activity 14:02** | The run was paused at step 3. |
| **Completed · 6 of 6** | Every step was marked done or skipped. **Completed · 6 of 6 (1 skipped)** says how many were skipped. |
| **Ended early · 2 of 6** | The run was ended, or replaced by a new run, before every step was closed. Bower also closes a run this way when its live session was lost and nobody came back to it. 2 of its 6 steps were done or skipped. |

Activity from today shows just the time; anything older shows the date too, such as **Oct 14, 14:02**. A run's version is tagged beside it, such as **v2**. Runs from before structured protocols are tagged **Earlier**.

### Can a finished run change?

No. Once a run is **Completed** or **Ended early**, it's a fixed record: its steps and times don't change. To do the protocol again, start a new run.

There's one exception, for a misheard last step. When Live marks the last step done and finishes the protocol, you have 10 minutes, in the same live session, to say *"go back"* or *"undo"*. Bower asks, for example, *"Reopen the finished protocol at step 6, Image the gel?"* and reopens it only when you say yes. See [Undoing the last step](/ai-and-search/live-agent-mode#undoing-the-last-step). After 10 minutes, or for a run that **Ended early**, start a new run instead.

### How do I see the runs of a protocol?

In the protocol library, each protocol with runs has a **Runs** count, such as **3 runs**, with **1 open** beside it when any run is still open. Click the count, or the arrow beside the protocol's name, to show its runs as rows underneath. Click it again to hide them. A protocol with an open run shows its runs straight away.

Each run row shows its number and dates (for example **Run 3 · Oct 13-14**), its version, its label, who ran it, and its last activity. Runs are listed newest first, 10 at a time; click **Show 10 more**, which also says how many are left, to load the next ones. When you scroll through a long list of runs, the protocol's own row stays pinned at the top so you know whose runs you're reading.

To list only protocols that have a run still going, turn on **Open runs** in the toolbar. It works with the search, the other filters and the sort.

With a keyboard, press the right arrow on a protocol's row to show its runs, and again to move to the first run. From a run, the left arrow goes back to its protocol; press it again to hide the runs.

You can also open a protocol's runs from its **⋮** menu with **View runs**.

On a protocol's own page, its runs are in the **Runs** section of the sidebar. See [Runs and versions on the protocol page](#runs-and-versions-on-the-protocol-page).

### What is on a run record?

Click a run to open its record. The top of the page shows the run number, who ran it and when, the protocol's title with **Open protocol**, the run's label, and a line such as **Ran Version 2 · 9 captures · 1 h 04 min**. **Ran Version 2 (draft)** means the run used a version that was still a draft at the time. For a run that's still open, the duration says **so far**.

The record has two tabs:

- **Steps** lists the steps exactly as they were run. Each step shows whether it was done, skipped or not done, and **Done 10:06** with the time Bower recorded it. Skipped steps show **Skipped** and no time. Beside each step are the captures taken for it, with their type and time, such as **Photo · 10:06:12**; click one to open it. A step with nothing captured says **No capture**, and captures that don't belong to any one step are listed together under **Not tied to a step**. On an open run, the current step is highlighted.
- **Timeline** puts the run in order: when it started, each capture, each step as it was marked done, and when the run completed or ended early. Skipped steps have no time, so they aren't on the timeline.

A summary beside the steps counts the steps done, the skipped steps and the captures, and shows the duration, the version and who ran it.

Notices at the top tell you when the record needs reading with care:

- **Steps changed since this run. The steps below are the ones that were run.** appears for a run on a draft when anyone has edited that draft's steps since.
- **The protocol is now Version 3.** means a newer version exists than the one this run used.
- **Earlier run** marks a run recorded before structured protocols. Its steps are shown as they were saved, but its sections and version aren't available.

Steps are grouped under their section names only when the version was already approved before the run started, because an approved version can't change. A run on a draft shows its steps as one list, since the draft's sections may have been renamed or regrouped since.

Times on a run record are the times Bower recorded on its servers. Runs from before step times were recorded show which steps were done, but not when.

### Runs and versions on the protocol page

On a protocol's page, the sidebar has a **Runs** section above **Versions**. Both work like the other sidebar sections: click the header to open or fold it, and the header shows a count.

- **Runs** shows how many runs the protocol has and when the latest was, such as **4 · last Oct 14**, or how many are open, such as **4 · 1 open**. It's folded unless a run is open, and hidden when the protocol has no runs. If the runs can't load, it says **Couldn't load runs.** with a way to retry.
- Open, it lists up to five runs of the version you're viewing. Turn on **All versions** to include runs of every version. Click a run to open its record, or **View all in Protocols** to see the protocol's runs in the library.
- In **Versions**, click a version's run count, such as **· 5 runs**, to show that version's runs in **Runs**.

The protocol's **⋮** menu has **View runs**, which opens the **Runs** section. It's unavailable while the runs are loading, if they couldn't load, or when there are none yet, and hovering says which.

## Can Bower answer questions about a protocol?

Yes. Ask Bower in the chat about a protocol and it reads the whole thing: its sections and steps, which steps ask for a capture, and every sidebar section, including the ones you added yourself. For example, ask "What are the safety notes for the PCR setup protocol?" or "What's in the Storage section?". See [Bower AI assistant](/ai-and-search/bower-ai-assistant).

## What happens when two people edit the same step?

If a colleague saves a change to a step while you're editing the same step, Bower doesn't overwrite either change. A banner appears on the step: click **Review** to compare the two, then **Keep mine** or **Use theirs**. Until you choose, your version isn't saved, and Bower asks before you leave the page.

## What if an import misses something?

An import can miss things: a table it couldn't read, or a whole part of the protocol such as the materials list. When it does, a notice at the top of the page lists what's missing, with the page of the source where it knows it. Check those parts against your original document.

If a protocol is longer than Bower can load at once, the page tells you it isn't the whole method.

## What about older protocols written as a document?

Some older protocols were written as a single document rather than as steps. To convert one, open it and click **Structure it**. Bower reads the document and builds a structured version alongside it. When it's ready, click **Open it**. The original document isn't changed.

## The protocol library

The protocol library lists every protocol in your workspace, with its title, collection, how many times it has been run, who created it, and when it was last modified. Tags and the created date are in the column picker. Structured protocols carry a **Steps** badge. Click a protocol's run count to show its runs underneath it; see [How do I see the runs of a protocol?](#how-do-i-see-the-runs-of-a-protocol). While an import runs, or if it found no steps or failed, the row says so.

Search, sort, and filter by collection, tag, or creator. Select several protocols to act on them together.

## Managing and exporting a protocol

A structured protocol's **⋮** menu holds access, version history, moving, deleting, and export to PDF or Word. See [Protocol menu and export](/organisation/protocol-menu-and-export).

## Linking protocols to notes

Protocols become more useful when linked to the notes they relate to. Use the [link entity](/organisation/linked-entities) feature to connect a protocol to relevant notes, samples, or collections. This makes it easy to trace which protocol was followed for a given set of observations.

## The Protocols page

The protocol library looks and works like the workspace's [List view](/organisation/organising-workspace#views).

- **One page for protocols and their runs.** Each protocol's runs open as rows beneath it, so there's no separate runs tab. See [Protocol runs](#protocol-runs). The old `/capture-stream` link opens this page.
- **The top bar** holds the workspace search and the **New protocol** button.
- **The toolbar** under the heading holds the protocol search box, the **Collection**, **Tags** and **Created by** filters, the **Open runs** filter, and a **Sort** menu.
- **Each row** shows the protocol's name, collection, runs, who created it, and when it was last modified.

### Finding your way back

When you open a protocol from the list, the top of its page shows a trail such as **Protocols / PCR setup**, with **in** and the protocol's collection beside it. Click the collection to open it in the workspace.

- **The back arrow** returns you to the list with your search, filters and sort as you left them.
- **Closing or deleting** the protocol also returns you to the list.
- **A run record** shows a trail such as **Protocols / PCR setup / Run 3**. Click the protocol's name to open the protocol. The back arrow returns you to where you came from, or to the protocol if you opened the run from a link.
- **Opened from a workspace collection**, or from a link, search or chat, the trail shows the collection path, as it does for any other item.

The trail appears on a computer. On a phone, protocol pages keep their usual navigation.
